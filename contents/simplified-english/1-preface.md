# Seeing Plural

> "In order to carry out a positive action we must develop here a positive vision… It is under the greatest adversity that there exists the greatest potential for doing good, both for oneself and others." — Dalai Lama XIV

---

The internet changed the world. Starting in the 1960s, it connected distant communities across space and time. Knowledge spread instantly across languages and cultures.

But globalization also widened gaps in wealth and status. Fast-moving digital technology gave rise to huge tech companies that pushed people into polarized groups.

The internet is powerful for connecting people across great differences. But it has also proven powerful for blocking those connections and creating new divisions.

It is no coincidence that democracy is now at a low point. Authoritarian regimes now control [nearly half](https://www.v-dem.net/documents/29/V-dem_democracyreport2023_lowres.pdf) of global GDP. Only about one billion people live under democracy, while over two billion live under authoritarian rule.[^VDem]

[^VDem]: V-Dem Institute, *Democracy Report 2023* (Gothenburg, Sweden: V-Dem Institute, 2023): 7.

Every culture has its own story, like a river. We see democracy as a river of hope. As its waters fall, we must refill it.

This book is a shared effort to restore that flow — and with it, hope.

In Mandarin, 數位 means both "digital" and "plural." To be plural is to be digital. To be digital is to be plural.

Plurality captures the connection between democracy and collaborative technology. Together, they can power endless diversity in endless combinations.

Let's free the future — together.
